Chapter 5 · Stories and False Coherence

Narrative Trap Machine

A visual lab showing how people convert partial evidence into a convincing story, then mistake coherence for truth.

Lab rule: A good story is not the same thing as good evidence.

The Question

When does explanation become overfitting? This lab asks students to build a story from partial clues, then introduces missing or contradictory evidence to test whether the story was adaptive sensemaking or premature closure.

Chapter 5 connection

Chapter 5 can examine the human need for coherent stories. Stories help us act, teach, remember, and coordinate, but they can also hide uncertainty and turn selected evidence into false confidence.

Guide to the Simulation

The machine begins with a messy evidence field. Some clues support a story, some contradict it, some are ambiguous, and some are missing. Students choose how strongly to favor coherence, speed, skepticism, and missing evidence. The system then shows how a narrative forms.

Confirming cluesEvidence that fits the emerging story.
Disconfirming cluesEvidence that resists the story.
Ambiguous cluesEvidence that can be interpreted in multiple ways.
Missing evidenceInformation not yet observed but still potentially important.
Teaching move: Ask students to make the story compelling, then ask what evidence would make the story less compelling.

Reflection Prompts

  • Which clues did the story absorb easily?
  • Which clues were ignored or explained away?
  • Did coherence rise faster than evidence coverage?
  • What missing evidence mattered most?
  • When is a story useful, and when does it become a trap?

Chapter Connection

This lab builds from the first four chapters. Students have already practiced deciding in uncertainty, seeing bias weather, resisting hindsight, and navigating fitness landscapes. Chapter 5 now adds the danger of narrative closure.

Adaptive stories should organize uncertainty, not erase it.
