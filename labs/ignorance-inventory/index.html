Chapter 13 · Productive Ignorance

Ignorance Inventory

A visual lab for turning unknowns into decision variables instead of hidden vulnerabilities.

Lab rule: Ignorance becomes dangerous when it stays invisible. It becomes adaptive when it is mapped, tested, and monitored.

The Question

What do we not know, and how much does that ignorance matter? This lab helps students classify unknowns by impact, knowability, urgency, and monitoring value. The goal is not to eliminate ignorance. The goal is to make ignorance usable.

Chapter 13 connection

Chapter 13 can serve as a practical capstone for adaptive judgment. After students have learned to decide, notice bias, resist hindsight, model change, manage attention, use AI carefully, and preserve optionality, this lab asks them to inventory what remains unknown.

How to use this lab

1. StartRun the default state and notice the baseline pattern.
2. ChangeMove one control at a time so the cause of the change stays visible.
3. StressUse the stress test, scenario button, or reset to compare worlds.
4. InterpretRead the dashboard before deciding what the result means.
5. TransferUse the reflection prompts to connect the pattern beyond the lab.

Pedagogical spine

Adaptive problemTurn unknowns into decision variables that can be tested, monitored, or accepted deliberately.
Misconception challengedIgnorance means either paralysis or reckless guessing.
Transfer targetSort unknowns by impact, learnability, urgency, and monitoring value before commitment.

How to interpret this lab

Do not read the map as a knowledge score. Read it as a priority map. Red blind spots require reframing or monitoring. Green unknowns invite experiments. Gold assumptions should not be treated as blue knowledge.

Guide to the Inventory

The map separates what is known, assumed, unknown, dangerous, and learnable. Students use sliders to shape the knowledge environment, then review which type of ignorance deserves action.

KnownsEvidence the decision maker can currently rely on.
AssumptionsClaims being treated as true before being fully tested.
Known unknownsImportant questions the decision maker can name.
Unknown unknownsRisks not yet visible because the frame is incomplete.
Teaching move: Ask students to rank unknowns by decision impact, not by intellectual curiosity alone.

Reflection Prompts

  • Which unknown has the highest impact?
  • Which assumption is being treated as knowledge?
  • Which unknown can be tested before commitment?
  • Which unknown must be monitored after action?
  • What could reveal that the whole frame is incomplete?

Chapter Connection

This lab works as a capstone because every adaptive system eventually reaches the edge of what it knows. The goal is to make that edge visible and actionable.

A mature decision system does not pretend to know everything. It knows how to live with, test, and update what it does not know.
