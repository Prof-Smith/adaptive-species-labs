Adaptive Judgment Lab · Chapter 2

Bias Weather Map

Bias is easier to teach when students can see the weather around the decision. This simplified lab uses three environmental forces to show when shortcuts become tempting.

The question

When does the environment make bias more likely?

Chapter 2 shifts the focus from flawed people to biased conditions. The same person can reason differently when pressure, ambiguity, and social pull change.

Lab rule:
Do not start by blaming the person. First inspect the weather around the decision.
Step 1

Set the decision weather

Only three forces matter in this simplified version: pressure, ambiguity, and social pull.

Step 2

Watch the bias weather form

The map shows environmental forces, not permanent personality flaws. Change one force and the likely shortcut changes.

Social proof

Ambiguity and social pull are high enough that other people’s behavior starts to feel like evidence.

  • Ambiguity makes private evidence feel incomplete.
  • Social pull makes the crowd easier to trust.
Protective move: ask for base ratesBefore following the crowd, ask what usually happens in similar situations.
