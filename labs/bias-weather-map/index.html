Chapter 2 · Bias as Weather

Bias Weather Map

A visual simulation showing how stress, ambiguity, incentives, social pressure, and loss framing create conditions where different biases become more likely.

Lab rule: Bias is not just inside the person. Bias is also in the weather around the decision.

The Question

Which decision conditions make judgment more vulnerable? This lab turns bias into a weather system. Instead of memorizing a list of biases, students change the environment and watch which distortions become more likely.

Chapter 2 connection

Chapter 2 can move beyond “people are biased” toward a more adaptive claim: judgment changes when the environment changes. Bias is often situational, patterned, and predictable.

Guide to the Simulation

The map shows a decision environment. Each slider changes the decision weather. When the weather worsens, certain biases intensify. Students should ask, “What does this environment select for?” rather than “What is wrong with the decision maker?”

StressRaises urgency and narrows attention.
Time pressureRewards fast shortcuts over slow comparison.
AmbiguityLeaves room for stories, assumptions, and projection.
Social proofMakes the crowd feel like evidence.
Loss framingMakes avoidance and panic more tempting.
Incentive pressureMakes motivated reasoning more attractive.
ConfidenceCan stabilize judgment or conceal missing evidence.
Feedback delayLets errors survive longer before correction.

How to interpret

A high score does not mean someone is doomed to be irrational. It means the environment is selecting for a shortcut. Adaptive judgment begins by changing the weather: slow the decision, seek disconfirming evidence, reduce incentive distortion, or create feedback.

Reflection Prompts

  • Which bias intensified first as the environment changed?
  • Which slider most changed the decision weather?
  • What would you change about the environment before blaming the person?
  • Which protective move would most improve judgment?
  • What is the difference between bias as a flaw and bias as an adaptation to conditions?

Chapter Connection

This lab prepares students for the rest of the book by showing that judgment is ecological. We do not only carry biases into decisions. We enter environments that amplify or dampen certain biases.

Better judgment often starts by redesigning the conditions around the decision.
