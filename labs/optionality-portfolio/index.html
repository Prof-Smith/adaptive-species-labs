Chapter 11 · Optionality and Reversibility

Optionality Portfolio Lab

A visual simulation showing how adaptive decision makers balance safety, upside, flexibility, and irreversible commitment under uncertain futures.

Lab rule: Optionality is not indecision. It is the design of choices that can learn from the future.

The Question

How should a decision maker allocate scarce resources when the future may branch in several directions? This lab turns optionality into a portfolio. Students choose how much to place in safety, upside, flexibility, and commitment, then see how the portfolio performs across possible futures.

Chapter 11 connection

Chapter 11 can connect adaptation to reversibility, staged commitment, and portfolio thinking. The goal is not to avoid commitments, but to avoid becoming trapped before the future reveals what kind of world has arrived.

Guide to the Simulation

The lab shows four allocation buckets. Each bucket has a different adaptive role. The simulation tests the resulting portfolio across base, boom, shock, and pivot scenarios.

Safety reserveProtects survival and buys time when conditions worsen.
Upside betsCreates large gains in favorable scenarios, but can disappoint in shocks.
Flexible optionsPreserves the ability to pivot when new information arrives.
Irreversible commitmentCan create focus and scale, but reduces future maneuverability.
Teaching move: Ask students which allocation is best before the scenario is known, then compare that with what looks best after the scenario is revealed.

Chapter Connection

This lab follows institutional inertia by asking how decision makers can design choices that remain adaptive across uncertain futures. Optionality is a response to uncertainty, not an escape from responsibility.

The adaptive question is not only what should I choose, but what future choices does this choice preserve?
