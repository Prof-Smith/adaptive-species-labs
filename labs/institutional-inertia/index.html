Chapter 10 · Institutions and Inertia

Institutional Inertia Simulator

A visual simulation showing how organizations can recognize change yet still adapt too slowly because proposals move through friction, delay, politics, and legacy commitments.

Lab rule: Institutions do not fail only because they choose poorly. They fail when they choose at the wrong speed.

The Question

Why do organizations struggle to adapt even when people inside them can see the need for change? This lab turns institutional adaptation into a pipeline. Environmental signals arrive, proposals form, committees review, budgets constrain, implementation lags, and outcomes arrive late.

Chapter 10 connection

Chapter 10 can shift the book from individual adaptation to organizational adaptation. The central issue is not only intelligence or motivation. It is whether the institution can convert information into action quickly enough.

Guide to the Simulation

The blue signal line represents environmental change. The orange proposal marker represents awareness inside the institution. Purple nodes represent review and approval friction. Green means implementation. Red means the opportunity window closed before action arrived.

Environmental signalChange outside the institution: students, markets, technology, regulation, or competitors.
Proposal formationThe organization notices the signal and creates a possible response.
FrictionCommittees, politics, budget rules, legacy commitments, and risk aversion slow movement.
Implementation lagThe time between decision and real-world adaptation.
Teaching move: Ask whether the organization failed because it lacked ideas, or because good ideas moved too slowly through the system.

Reflection Prompts

  • When does process protect quality, and when does process become avoidance?

Chapter Connection
