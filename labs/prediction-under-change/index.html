Chapter 6 · Evolution at the Speed of Thought

Prediction Under Change

An Arcas-inspired artificial life lab: compare random, reactive, and predictive agents inside a moving opportunity landscape.

Lab rule: Intelligence is not certainty. It is the ability to model change before change finishes happening.
Adaptive Prediction Lab

The Question

What happens when the world changes faster than a purely reactive agent can follow? Agents search for energy in a changing environment. Some wander randomly, some follow the best current signal, and some use recent change to predict where opportunity may move next.

Why Chapter 6?

Chapter 6 is about mental simulation, imagination, and testing futures before reality imposes full cost. This lab gives students a visual model of prediction as adaptive anticipation.

Guide to the Simulation

This lab is inspired by Blaise Agüera y Arcas’s argument that prediction is fundamental not only to intelligence and the brain, but to life itself. Public descriptions of What Is Intelligence? say the book connects AI, evolution, computing, and minds, and describes intelligence across molecules, organisms, societies, and AI. The lab uses that idea as a teaching model, not as an exact recreation of his code.

Random agents

Gray agents move without using environmental information. They represent behavior with no model of where opportunity is or where it is going.

Reactive agents

Blue agents move toward the strongest current signal. They adapt to the present, but do not anticipate where the environment is moving.

Predictive agents

Red agents combine the current signal with recent change. They attempt to move toward where opportunity appears to be emerging next.

How to interpret

Stable, slow worlds can reward reactive behavior because today’s signal remains useful. Changing, patterned worlds can reward prediction because recent movement contains information. Highly noisy worlds can punish overprediction.

Random: no model
Reactive: follows current signal
Predictive: follows current signal plus recent change

Reflection Prompts

  • When does prediction outperform reaction?
  • When does prediction become too costly or too confident?
  • How does volatility change what counts as intelligence?
  • What would the human version of movement cost be?
  • How does this lab connect to AI as a tool for anticipation?

Chapter Connection

This lab sits before the Axelrod cooperation lab. Chapter 6 asks how minds simulate futures before reality tests them. Chapter 8 asks how strategies survive when other agents adapt back. Together, the labs separate predictive ecology from strategic ecology.

Prediction is not a guarantee. It is an adaptive wager about where the world is moving.
