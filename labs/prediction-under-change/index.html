Chapter 6 · Evolution at the Speed of Thought

Prediction Under Change

An Arcas-inspired artificial life lab: compare random, reactive, and predictive agents inside a moving opportunity landscape.

Lab rule: Intelligence is not certainty. It is the ability to model change before change finishes happening.

The Question

What happens when the world changes faster than a purely reactive agent can follow? Agents search for energy in a changing environment. Some wander randomly, some follow the best current signal, and some use recent change to predict where opportunity may move next.

Why Chapter 6?

This lab gives students a visual model of prediction as adaptive anticipation: thought rehearses the future before reality imposes the full cost.

How to use this lab

1. StartRun the default state and notice the baseline pattern.
2. ChangeMove one control at a time so the cause of the change stays visible.
3. StressUse the stress test, scenario button, or reset to compare worlds.
4. InterpretRead the dashboard before deciding what the result means.
5. TransferUse the reflection prompts to connect the pattern beyond the lab.

Pedagogical spine

Adaptive problemDecide when anticipation beats reaction in an environment that moves before certainty arrives.
Misconception challengedPrediction is always better than reaction.
Transfer targetAsk whether the environment has enough pattern to make prediction useful and enough stability to avoid overprediction.

How to interpret this lab

Track the population bars while watching the opportunity rings move. Prediction should help when motion has pattern, but reactive agents may survive in slower worlds and random agents may persist through mutation.

Quick modeStart the simulation and identify which agent type becomes dominant under default conditions.
Deep modeCompare low volatility with high volatility, then change prediction weight to find when prediction becomes too aggressive.

How to interpret

Stable worlds can reward reaction. Changing, patterned worlds can reward prediction. Noisy worlds can punish overprediction.

Reflection Prompts

  • When did predictive agents gain ground?
  • When did reactive agents remain competitive?
  • What made prediction costly?
  • How did volatility change the value of intelligence?
  • What human decision resembles this moving landscape?

Prompts are designed to move from observation to transfer.
