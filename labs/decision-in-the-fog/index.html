The question

How do you act before the ending is known?

Chapter 1 begins with the human condition: decisions arrive before certainty. The goal is not to guess the future correctly. The goal is to preserve the quality of judgment before the future answers.

Lab rule:
Do not judge the decision from the ending. Reconstruct the mind that had to act before the ending was known.
Step 1

Use only three controls. They define the decision weather: how much evidence exists, how quickly the window closes, and how serious the consequences are.

Step 2

See the fog map

No choice is automatically a good or weak process. The selected path first moves through the environment, then process quality is judged by fit, preparation, and timing. The four outcome branches show where that process can land.

Environment: mixed signal · narrowing window · meaningful stakes

Path A · Commit

Strong with clear evidence and costly delay; weak when commitment outruns what is known.

Path B · Stage

Strong when staging preserves learning and reversibility; weak if staging becomes disguised delay.

Path C · Wait

Strong when waiting produces new evidence; weak when waiting hides uncertainty or misses the window.

Interpretation rule: A path does not determine process quality by itself. Commit, Stage, and Wait can each be disciplined or undisciplined depending on the environment and whether the choice preserves learning.
