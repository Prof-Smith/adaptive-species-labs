Chapter 1 · The Species That Decides

Decision in the Fog

Make a decision before certainty arrives, then learn without letting the ending rewrite the quality of the judgment.

Lab rule: Do not judge the decision from the ending. Reconstruct the mind that had to act before the ending was known.

The Question

How do you preserve judgment when you must act before the ending is known? This lab creates a foggy decision environment: partial facts, visible assumptions, hidden unknowns, risk zones, and reversible or irreversible paths.

Chapter 1 connection

Chapter 1 introduces the human condition of acting before certainty arrives. This lab lets students experience that problem and then separate process quality from outcome quality.

How to use this lab

1. StartRun the default state and notice the baseline pattern.
2. ChangeMove one control at a time so the cause of the change stays visible.
3. StressUse the stress test, scenario button, or reset to compare worlds.
4. InterpretRead the dashboard before deciding what the result means.
5. TransferUse the reflection prompts to connect the pattern beyond the lab.

Pedagogical spine

Adaptive problemAct before certainty arrives while preserving the pre-outcome evidence, assumptions, and unknowns.
Misconception challengedA good outcome proves a good decision, and a bad outcome proves a bad decision.
Transfer targetUse a decision snapshot before major commitments so learning is not corrupted by the ending.

How to interpret this lab

If the process score rises while the outcome remains mixed, students should see the central lesson: judgment quality and outcome quality are related but not identical. Watch how clarity, optionality, and overconfidence reshape the decision before the result is revealed.

Quick modeUse the default scenario, choose one path, reveal the outcome, and place the result in the Four Rooms of Judgment.
Deep modeRun two contrasting environments: one with high clarity and optionality, one with low clarity and high pressure. Compare how the same outcome feels different after the pre-outcome map is completed.

Guide to the Lab

Known facts

Blue markers represent information that is more reliable.

Assumptions

Yellow markers represent beliefs that may or may not hold.

Fog / unknowns

Gray fog represents missing information that could matter later.

Risk zones

Red zones show potential harm or downside exposure.

Opportunity zones

Green zones show possible upside or learning value.

Update flags

Purple flags mark evidence that should make you reconsider.

Teaching move: Ask students to record what they knew before the outcome. Then reveal the result and ask whether hindsight is changing their judgment.
