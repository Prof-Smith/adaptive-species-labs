Chapter 8 · Games

Cooperation Under Selection

Modify repeated-game strategies, run an Axelrod-style tournament, and watch cooperation rise, stabilize, or collapse through ecological selection.

Lab rule: No strategy wins alone. Every strategy is selected inside a game.

The Question

What kind of game allows cooperation to survive? Compare short-run tournament success with long-run ecological survival. The lab lets students manipulate the conditions that make cooperation more or less adaptive.

Axelrod’s Four Rules

  1. Don’t be envious.
  2. Don’t be the first to defect.
  3. Reciprocate both cooperation and defection.
  4. Don’t be too clever.

Guide to the Game

The Prisoner’s Dilemma creates a tension between individual temptation and mutual gain. In a single round, defection dominates: if the other player cooperates, defection pays more; if the other player defects, defection still pays more. Cooperation becomes possible only when players meet repeatedly and the future has enough weight to discipline the present.

How to read the matrix

First number: your payoff. Second number: the other player’s payoff. The tension is visible in the orange cells: defection pays more in the immediate round, but mutual cooperation produces better joint value over time.

The temptation payoff updates when you change T below, so students can see how a stronger short-term incentive changes the ecology.

Temptation payoff T

This is the reward for exploiting a cooperator. Raising T makes defection more attractive. In classroom terms, it asks: how large must the short-term gain become before cooperation breaks?

Accidental flips / noise

Noise randomly flips intended moves. A cooperative strategy may accidentally defect, or a defector may accidentally cooperate. Noise tests whether a strategy is brittle or forgiving enough to recover from mistakes.

Shadow of the future δ

The discount factor says how much later rounds count. When δ is high, future consequences matter. When δ is low, the game becomes more short-term and defection becomes more tempting.

The weighting function

The lab weights each round using the discount factor:

Weighted payoff = p₁ + δp₂ + δ²p₃ + ... + δT−1pT

The site reports weighted averages so students can compare results across different numbers of rounds and different discount settings.

Run the Ecology

Use the controls to change the population ecology. Then compare the tournament ranking with the final ecological population share.

What the Stress Test does

Stress Test: Short Future + Defection intentionally makes cooperation harder. It sets noise to 6%, lowers δ to 0.70, raises the temptation payoff to 7, and turns My Strategy into a defection-first rule.

  • More noise: mistakes can trigger retaliation.
  • Shorter future: later consequences matter less.
  • Higher temptation: exploiting cooperation pays more.
  • Defection first: the custom strategy begins by poisoning trust.
