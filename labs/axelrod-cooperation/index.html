Chapter 8 · Games

Cooperation Under Selection

Modify repeated-game strategies, run an Axelrod-style tournament, and watch cooperation rise, stabilize, or collapse through ecological selection.

Lab rule: No strategy wins alone. Every strategy is selected inside a game.

How to use this lab

1. StartRun the default tournament and notice the baseline ecology.
2. ChangeAdjust noise, temptation, or the shadow of the future one at a time.
3. StressUse the short-future stress test to see cooperation collapse or shift.
4. InterpretCompare tournament ranking with final ecological share.
5. TransferConnect cooperation to institutions, trust, and repeated relationships.

Pedagogical spine

Adaptive problemUnderstand when cooperation survives among strategies that adapt to one another.
Misconception challengedCooperation survives because people simply prefer to be nice.
Transfer targetLook for incentives, memory, time horizons, forgiveness, and error correction in any cooperative system.

How to interpret this lab

Compare tournament rank with final ecological share. A strategy can score well in pairwise play but fail to dominate the ecology once selection, noise, and the shadow of the future interact.

Quick modeRun the default, then run the stress test and compare the top strategy and final ecology.
Deep modeChange one condition at a time: noise, temptation, or discount factor. Ask which strategies are nice, retaliatory, forgiving, and clear.

Guide to the Game

The Prisoner’s Dilemma creates tension between short-term temptation and long-term mutual gain. Cooperation becomes possible when players meet repeatedly and the future matters.
